# Pre-purchase inspection basics

Cold start: ask the seller not to warm up the engine. Listen for knocking, ticking or long cranking. White smoke that persists may indicate coolant issues; blue smoke suggests oil burning; black smoke suggests rich fuel mixture.

Body and paint: look for uneven panel gaps, paint colour mismatch between panels, overspray on rubber seals and trims, and fresh underseal. These can be signs of past repair or repaint. Check for rust at door bottoms, wheel arches and under the boot mat.

Tyres: uneven wear on one edge can indicate alignment or suspension problems. Check manufacture date (DOT code) on the sidewall; tyres older than 5-6 years should be replaced regardless of tread depth.

Odometer and documents: compare odometer reading with service records and insurance history. Verify the chassis number (VIN) on the body against the RC book. Check that the registered owner matches the seller and that there is no pending loan hypothecation or challan.

Brakes and suspension: brake pedal should feel firm without pulsation. Pulling to one side while braking or clunking over bumps suggests worn components. Bounce each corner of the car; it should settle in one rebound.

Service and maintenance: regular service at authorised or reputable workshops every 10,000 km or 12 months is typical for cars. Missing records raise uncertainty about oil changes, timing belt/chain and clutch wear. Ask for invoices, not just stamped service books.

Insurance and claims: ask for the current insurance policy and claim history. A high no-claim bonus percentage usually suggests few claims. Total-loss or flood-damaged vehicles may have electrical faults and musty interiors.

Two-wheelers: check chain and sprocket wear, fork seal leaks, engine oil leaks, brake pad thickness, and frame or handlebar alignment. Verify engine and chassis numbers against RC.

Always get an independent mechanic inspection and an OBD scan (for cars with OBD ports) before paying any token amount.
